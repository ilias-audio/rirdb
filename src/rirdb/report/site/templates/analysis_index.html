What was analysed, and how

Each analysed IR is read from the original files, cropped at its onset (ISO 3382-1: 20 dB below the peak) and filtered into octave bands from 63 Hz to 8 kHz. The analyzer computes the decay times (T30, T20, EDT), the energy ratios (C50, C80, D50, centre time), the direct-to-reverberant ratio, STI, bass and treble ratios and the mixing time. A value counts only where the decay range allows it, so every value has a validity flag, and every IR gets a grade from A to D. A separate signal audit looks for recording problems (sweep residue, hum, dropouts, unsteady noise, resampling) and raises flags; see Signal quality. The full method is on the Methods page.

The analysed datasets compared

One row per analysed dataset ({{ n_rows }}), sorted by the number of IRs analysed. Click a column heading to sort. Values are medians unless the heading says otherwise.

Which IRs. The grades count every analysed IR. T30, EDT, C80, DRR, STI and the T30 valid share use the IRs of real spaces (rooms, outdoor sites, vehicles; no anechoic, modelled, device or scale-model IRs), one representation per position, duplicates removed.{% for r in cmp_rows if r.over %} {{ r.name }} has no IRs of real spaces: its values are over its {{ r.over }} IRs, which the index still files as real spaces; its T30 is marked {{ r.over }}.{% endfor %} Peak-to-noise, bandwidth and duplicates use every analysed IR.

T30 is the mean of the 500 Hz and 1 kHz octaves, where both are valid. The bar spans the 10th to 90th percentile on a log scale from 0.1 to 10 s, the dot is the median. Where fewer than half of those IRs allow a T30, the T30-or-T20 value is shown and marked T20. T30 valid: share of those IRs where T30 is valid at both 500 Hz and 1 kHz.

Peak-to-noise (PNR): the usable decay range of the recording. Bandwidth: where the content of the IR stops, which can be far below the file's Nyquist frequency. Duplicates: share of IRs that are sample-identical copies of another IR.

T30 validity per octave band

ISO 3382-1 fits T30 on the decay from −5 to −35 dB, and the bottom of that range must stay at least 10 dB above the noise floor. So a band has a valid T30 only when its decay range, from the smoothed peak to the noise, is at least 45 dB (35 dB for T20, 20 dB for EDT). The analyzer enforces this itself. Low bands fail most often: the octave filters ring, the noise is higher, and short files end before the decay does.

Three ways to count it, over the {{ validity.n_irs | int }} clean measured-room IRs of {{ validity.n_datasets | int }} datasets and {{ validity.n_spaces | int }} spaces. The per-IR share is dominated by the largest datasets; the per-dataset median gives each dataset one vote; the per-space share says whether a space has at least one usable IR in that band.
