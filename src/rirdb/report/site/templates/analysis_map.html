What the map is

The map is fitted once and then frozen, so a dataset or a new set of IRs always lands in the same place. It has two versions, one per CLAP signal:

  • The IR itself: the embedding of the impulse response as audio.
  • Effect on dry recordings (reverb_vector): the embedding of what the IR does to six dry recordings, the convolved audio, averaged over the six. This is closer to how a listener meets the reverb.

Distances are cosine distances in the full embedding space; the flat UMAP picture is only for looking. UMAP places points that are unlike anything in the map next to their nearest neighbours, so a set can look closer to the corpus than it is. Read the numbers, not the picture.

The map sample keeps preferred, de-duplicated IRs without analysis errors, at most {{ M.cap_per_dataset | int }} per dataset and at least one per room. Realism and coverage use only its clean measured-room IRs.

Realism and coverage

Neighbourhood of a reference IR
The distance from a clean measured-room IR to its {{ M.k }}th-nearest neighbour from another dataset. Other datasets only, because CLAP groups IRs by their recording chain: an IR's nearest neighbour is almost always from its own dataset, and same-dataset neighbourhoods would call every new set unrealistic.
Realism
Share of a set's IRs that fall inside the neighbourhood of at least one reference IR. In the spirit of precision (Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019).
Coverage
Share of the reference IRs, and of the reference rooms, with at least one of the set's IRs inside their neighbourhood. In the spirit of recall and coverage (Naeem et al. 2020).
Yardstick
Each real dataset is scored against all the others, with its own IRs left out of every neighbourhood. This is exactly how a new set is scored, so the real datasets' scores show what “real” looks like.
