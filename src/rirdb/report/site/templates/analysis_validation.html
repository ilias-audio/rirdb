OpenAIR: published per-space tables

OpenAIR published a table of acoustic parameters for many of its spaces. rirdb compares its own values with those tables for the {{ O.n_spaces | int }} spaces that have both a table and analysed IRs. rirdb's value for a space is the median over the space's preferred IRs of the valid values (Lundeby-compensated decay curves). OpenAIR did not document its method or which IRs it used, so perfect agreement is not expected. OpenAIR's “Reverberation Time” row is compared with T30.

A median ratio of 1 or a median difference of 0 means no bias. From 250 Hz to 4 kHz the T30 median ratio is between {{ O.t30_ratio_range[0] | num(2) }} and {{ O.t30_ratio_range[1] | num(2) }}, and {{ O.t30_within_range[0] | pct }} to {{ O.t30_within_range[1] | pct }} of the spaces agree within 10 %. EDT, C80 and D50 depend on where the source and microphone stood, and OpenAIR gives one value per space, so they scatter more.

ACE challenge: ground truth per IR

The ACE challenge published, for each measured IR, its own ISO T30 (broadband and per band) and a broadband direct-to-reverberant ratio. rirdb compares its values with them on the multichannel arrays: {{ A.n_irs | int }} IRs ({{ A.configs | length }} arrays, {{ A.configs | join(", ") }}, at {{ A.n_room_pos | int }} positions in {{ A.rooms | length }} rooms{% if A.n_spaces and A.n_spaces > A.rooms | length %}; rirdb indexes {{ A.n_spaces | int }} spaces for this dataset, and the comparison keeps the IRs whose room, position and array match a ground-truth row{% endif %}), {{ A.n_metrics }} parameters each: {{ A.n_points | int }} comparisons. rirdb's first channel is compared with ACE's channel 1. ACE's per-band values are in 1/3 octaves, compared here with rirdb's octave bands.

DRR in rirdb follows ACE's own convention: the full-band IR with a direct window of ±8 ms around the direct peak. ACE's measurement file states that window in every row. rirdb first used a narrower window, which put its DRR consistently below ACE's; the comparison here is what led to the change.

Known limitation: C80 at 125 Hz

{% if openair and openair.lim %}

At 125 Hz, rirdb's C80 is {{ (-openair.lim.median_diff) | num(1) }} dB lower than OpenAIR's (median over {{ openair.lim.n | int }} spaces; mean absolute difference {{ openair.lim.mae | num(1) }} dB).{% if openair.c80_250 %} At 250 Hz the median difference is {{ openair.c80_250.median_diff | num(1) }} dB{% if openair.c80_hi_max is not none %}; from 500 Hz up it stays within ±{{ openair.c80_hi_max | num(1) }} dB{% endif %}.{% endif %}

The likely cause is the group delay of the causal octave filter at low frequencies, which moves early energy past the 80 ms boundary. OpenAIR did not document its filters. Decay times are not affected: a pure delay does not change a slope. The candidate fix for the next analyzer version is to compensate each band for its group delay at the centre frequency before the energy ratios are computed.

Until then, treat the early-energy parameters in the lowest bands as biased low when comparing with other tools{% if openair.d50_125 %} (D50 at 125 Hz: median difference {{ openair.d50_125.median_diff | num(2) }}){% endif %}. The analyzer's method notes are on the Methods page.
